# EDUARDO sopalda: Pre-Launch Audit
Repository: github.com/EduardoSopalda/eduardosopalda-web- · Audited 4 October 2026 · Astro 7 build, tested at 1440×900 and 390×844

## Verdict

The concept is strong and, in places, quietly brilliant. A true-black void, a single luminous "nerve" thread, and glowing specimens (octopus, goldfish, piglet, eye-fish) that drift past as rooms. That gives the site a real point of view: a Wunderkammer where data is the curiosity. The writing voice ("I don't make AI sound magical. I make it operational.") is better than most consultancy sites. The site is not ready to launch yet. Three blockers come first: a security leak, a hosting mismatch, and the domain's SSL. After that come a short set of craft fixes that would take it from "beautiful" to "unforgettable".

Scores (out of 10): Concept 9 · Art direction 8 · Motion 7 · Typography 6 · Mobile 5 · Performance 4 · Launch readiness 3

## 1. Launch blockers (fix first)

| # | Issue | Evidence | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Private Berlin material is public | The repo is public. It contains `private/berlin/film.mp4`, `roadmap.pdf` and the stills, and the desk key `estrel-desk-9c2` is hardcoded in `src/data/berlin.ts`. Anyone can download the embargoed film today straight from GitHub. | Make the repo private or delete `private/` from it and purge the history (git filter-repo / BFG). ## 2. Art direction

**What works**
- The palette discipline (`--void #000`, bone, bronze, champagne, oxide-lit) is rare and confident. The AA contrast fixes documented in `tokens.css` show real care.
- Compositing the specimens on pure black with no masks is the right call. They feel lit from within, like objects in a dark vitrine.
- Inner rooms (Context, Who, Lexicon) keep the same frame: a "FIELD NOTES" kicker, a serif title and an italic subtitle. That feels like one museum, not a series of templates.

**What to push**
1. **The cabinet has no cabinet yet.** The landing is a void with a thread. The metaphor only arrives once you are in the field. Add one architectural gesture: the faint edge of a vitrine, a drawer line, or a museum label tag on every specimen ("No. 07 · Octopus vulgaris · on distributed intelligence"). You are an architect, so let the space be drawn.
2. **The specimens need captions that pay off.** Right now they are beautiful but mute. Each creature should stand for an idea. The octopus is distributed cognition and agents, the goldfish is memory and retention, the eye-fish is observability. Show a hover or tap label in IBM Plex Mono in the style of a natural-history catalogue entry.
3. **Inner pages lose the magic.** Writing, Who and Lexicon go back to flat black with text. Carry one ambient element from the field into every room: the nerve thread as a hairline divider that glows as you scroll, or the room's own specimen breathing in the corner.
4. **Mixed image families.** The cinematic specimens sit next to Zoom screenshots (DataNext on Lexicon) and LinkedIn-style cards (Community). Treat every photo as an "exhibit": a duotone in bronze over black, the same frame, and a catalogue number. This one rule will make the site feel curated rather than collected.

## 3. Visual and video design

- **Video weight.** `public/specimens/` is 37 MB. The heaviest files are `seaweed.webm` (8.2 MB), `jellyfish.webm` (7.5 MB) and `goldfish2.webm` (4.0 MB). The home page transfers about 6.4 MB. Target under 1.5 MB on first view: re-encode to AV1/VP9 at 720p and CRF 38–42, cap each loop at 4–6 s, and serve a 200 KB poster frame first. Load only the specimens near the viewport.
- **Loop seams.** Seamless loops are what make specimens feel alive rather than played. Check each file for a visible jump at the loop point, and ping-pong or crossfade the last 12 frames.
- **Large PNGs.** `chimp.png` is 2.8 MB and `eyefish.png` 1.6 MB, and `public/cabinet` holds 11 MB of PNG cutouts. Convert them to WebP or AVIF with alpha (typically 80–90% smaller).
- **The Berlin film** is the strongest video asset. After 27 October, give it a proper "screening room" on Out Loud: a 16:9 frame in letterbox black, a title card, and a chapters list. Don't just embed a player.
- **OG image.** There is one `og-image.png` for the home page only, and inner pages have no `og:image`. Generate one per article from the article's illustration and title, so every LinkedIn share looks like an exhibit card. Most of your traffic will come from LinkedIn.

## 4. Mechanics (interaction and system)

- **Entry friction.** After load, the landing shows only the thread for about 2.5 s. The name arrives at about 5 s. Scrolling does nothing until you click "unroll the logic". Many visitors will scroll, see nothing happen, and leave.
  - Bring the name in by 1.2 s.
  - Let the first wheel, swipe or arrow key also "unroll" the field.
  - Make the cue pulse once at 3 s if the visitor has not acted yet.
- **Navigation model.** The field is pointer, wheel and keyboard driven, which is good. But there is no sense of where you are among the seven stops. Add a minimal progress rail (seven dots on the nerve, the current one lit) and support deep links (`/#lexicon`).
- **Monolith risk.** `Field.astro` is 3,831 lines (204 KB) with a single script. That makes it fragile to change before launch. After launch, split it into drift, decay, nerve, specimens and input modules.
- **SEO and sharing basics.** Inner pages have no `meta description` and no `og:*` tags. There is no `sitemap.xml` and no `robots.txt`. The Writing index has two `<h1>`s. Add `@astrojs/sitemap`, per-page descriptions and one H1 per page.
- **Reduced motion.** It is handled in Field and Landing. Make sure the decay text and the specimen videos also freeze (show the posters).
- **Analytics.** There are none. Add privacy-friendly analytics (Plausible or Vercel Analytics) and track one event: "entered the field". That number tells you if the landing works.

## 5. Animation

**What works.** The nerve thread has lovely organic easing, and the travelling light pulses read as data in motion. The wordmark's landing-to-corner morph is a smart signature gesture.

**What to fix**
1. **Decay overlaps legibility.** While a fragment dissolves, its letters scatter on top of the next quote ("And yes, there was a goat." sits under a scrambled line). Give each fragment its own exit lane. Dissolve upward and out, about 600 ms, and delay the next one by about 200 ms so the two never share pixels.
2. **Wordmark morph glitch.** In mid-transition (about 1.5 s after the click) "EDUARDO" and "sopalda" briefly overlap in the corner as a tangled glyph. Crossfade the opacity of the outgoing and incoming marks over about 150 ms, or animate a single element with FLIP.
3. **Mobile double name.** At 390 px, the large hero name and the docked corner wordmark are visible at the same time. Hide the corner mark until the hero has left.
4. **Timing bible.** Write down three tempos and use them everywhere:
   - "breath": 4–6 s, for specimens and the idle thread
   - "gesture": 600–900 ms, for room transitions and fragment exits
   - "tick": 120–180 ms, for hovers and labels

   Right now everything drifts at a similar slow tempo, which reads as calm but can tip into sleepy. Contrast makes motion memorable.
5. **Give the specimens acting.** Let them react softly to the cursor: the octopus curls a tentacle towards the pointer, and the eye-fish's pupil tracks it. One reactive creature is worth ten ambient ones. People will tell others about it.

## 6. Typography

**Families loaded:** Fraunces Variable, Newsreader Variable, Italiana, Glacial Indifference, IBM Plex Mono and Space Grotesk. That is six families, plus Times and Arial showing up as computed fallbacks.

- **Too many voices.** Six families for a site with this restraint dilutes the identity and adds font weight. Recommended system:
  - Display and body serif: Fraunces only. Drop Newsreader, and use Fraunces' optical sizes and soft axis for long reading.
  - Labels and catalogue: IBM Plex Mono only. Drop Space Grotesk.
  - Wordmark only: Italiana and Glacial, kept as a deliberate exception, as your design system already says.
- **Fallback leakage.** "Times New Roman" shows up in computed styles on every page, so some element is missing a family stack. Audit it with DevTools. Also add `size-adjust`/`ascent-override` fallbacks so text does not jump when the web fonts load.
- **Italic oxide body copy.** Long italic paragraphs in oxide-lit on black (the Context and Who intros) are tiring to read. Use roman bone for paragraphs over two lines, and keep italic oxide for short subtitles and pull quotes.
- **Tracking.** Spaced caps on the field labels (W R I T I N G) work at that size. "LEXICON" is clipped at the right edge on mobile, so add safe-area padding or let the label flip to the left of its node.
- **Measure.** Article body sits at about 60–68 characters, which is good. The Writing hero title breaks to four lines at 1440 px ("Ten Thousand / Files, Two / Thousand That / Mattered"). Allow the title column to be wider or reduce the clamp maximum.

## 7. Making it memorable

1. **A catalogue number for everything.** Every article, talk, specimen and photo gets an accession number (e.g. "ES-2026-025"). It is consistent with the "No.25" you already use. It turns content into a collection, and visitors will start to feel they are browsing your archive.
2. **"Take one with you."** At the end of an article, let readers "pocket" a fragment: a generated exhibit card (quote, specimen, number) they can download or post to LinkedIn. This is your best organic growth loop.
3. **A hidden drawer.** Cabinets of curiosities always had a secret compartment. Add one Easter egg, for example typing "goat" or clicking the nerve seven times, that opens a drawer with an architecture sketch from your early career. It links the architect to the data person in one moment of delight.
4. **Sound, optional and off by default.** A faint room tone and a soft glass "tick" when a specimen is touched. A tiny "sound on" toggle in Plex Mono. Done well, this is what people remember.
5. **The thread connects your 20 years.** Let the nerve on Who become a timeline: Barcelona → Tokyo → Lima → New York → dsm-firmenich. Light travels along it as you scroll. This is your story told in the site's own visual language.
6. **A Spanish/Catalan layer.** "Todo el mundo es un país" is already in the archive. A bilingual toggle on fragments would add depth for your Barcelona/Madrid audience.
