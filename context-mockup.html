01 / Before the departments

A room for
the almost
unbelievable.

In early modern Europe, a Wunderkammer—a chamber of wonder—or Kunstkammer gathered natural things, human-made marvels and instruments under one roof.

Shell beside astrolabe. Fossil beside carved ivory. Not a catalogue of the strange: a small, imperfect world assembled to be looked at, argued with, and understood.

02 / When the shelves divided

Then the world
got labelled.

Over time, collections became museums; inquiry acquired disciplines, departments and names. The sorting made knowledge more exact—and easier to share.

But a label can arrive too soon. Sometimes the most useful thing is to leave two unlike objects together long enough for a third idea to appear.

04 / What the cabinet is for

The meaning is
in the between.

My work moves across architecture and data, technology and people, systems and stories. I’m interested in what happens when unlike things meet—and in the meaning we make there, together.

Not every connection is a solution. Some are questions you can live with. Some change shape when another person enters the room.

A collection is never the whole world.
It is one way of seeing it.
